First: why we trusted the market

Three claims, all about the same thing — that resources end up where they are wanted.
01

Allocative

resources go to the goods consumers value most highly — spending is the vote

02

Productive

each good is made at the lowest possible average cost — waste is competed away

03

Dynamic

products and methods keep improving, because a rival is always close behind

All three rest on one foundation: the price mechanism.

So: market failure

Market failure occurs when the price mechanism fails to allocate resources efficiently.

IGCSE 2.9 · in other words, the market’s answer to “how much?” is not society’s answer

Not a collapse

goods are still made and sold. The market is working — to the wrong quantity.

Not greed

the firm answers the question it was asked, with a price that is missing something.

Name it: an external cost

Private cost

the cost borne by the firm or consumer that takes the decision

External cost

a cost imposed on third parties who are not part of the transaction

Do not write social when you mean external. Social cost includes the firm’s own cost as well.
SOCIAL COST = private cost + external cost

The other direction

The city’s rubbish is burned to make electricity. Neighbours breathe cleaner air — and pay nothing.
Without it: rubbish on the street
The plant burns it to make electricity

Now the bystander gains. The firm still cannot charge them, so the benefit never shows up in the price.

Name it: an external benefit

Private benefit

the benefit received by the buyer or firm that takes the decision

External benefit

a benefit received by third parties who are not part of the transaction

Do not write social when you mean external. Social benefit includes the buyer’s own gain as well.
SOCIAL BENEFIT = private benefit + external benefit
