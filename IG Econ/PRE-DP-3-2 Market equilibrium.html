DISEQUILIBRIUM · 失衡

When the price is wrong: shortage & surplus

A market is in disequilibrium when Qd and Qs do not match — the gap does the pushing.

Below ¥30 → a shortage

Qd is bigger than Qs: excess demand. Some buyers miss out, so the price is pushed UP.

Above ¥30 → a surplus

Qs is bigger than Qd: excess supply. Cups go unsold, so sellers cut the price DOWN.

The size of the gap is the size of the push. Only at ¥30 is the gap zero — nothing pushes, and the price stays put.

BACK TO EQUILIBRIUM · 回归

How the market gets back to ¥30

Exam favourite: explain how the market returns to ¥30. The arrows move along the curves — the curves themselves do not move.

Starting ABOVE ¥30 — a surplus

1 · At ¥40: Qs (7) > Qd (3) → 4 cups unsold.

2 · Unsold stock → sellers cut the price.

3 · Price falls → Qd extends, Qs contracts.

4 · Gap closes at ¥30 — market clears.

Starting BELOW ¥30 — a shortage

1 · At ¥20: Qd (7) > Qs (3) → 4 buyers miss out.

2 · Buyers bid up → sellers raise the price.

3 · Price rises → Qd contracts, Qs extends.

4 · Gap closes at ¥30 — market clears.

THE PRICE MECHANISM · 机制

Price does the organising

Nobody plans it. The price carries the message, and resources follow.

It is a signal — and an incentive

If buyers want more, the price rises. A higher price means more profit, so firms produce more — and they hire more labour, capital and land to do it.

It also rations

When supply falls short, the price is pushed up — and the people who end up with the product are those able to pay the higher price.

That is how a market answers the three big questions: what to produce, how to produce it, and for whom.

REVISION · 回顾

What shifts D — and what shifts S

Straight from 2-5 Demand and 3-1 Supply. Try to list them before you reveal.

Two lists, out loud: first the conditions of demand, then the conditions of supply. Reveal once you have both.

Conditions of DEMAND → shift D

· Income

· Tastes and fashion

· Price of substitutes and complements

· Number of buyers (population)

· Expectations of future prices

Conditions of SUPPLY → shift S

· Costs of production (wages, materials, rent, energy)

· Technology

· Government: indirect tax ↑ / subsidy ↓

· Weather and other shocks

· Number of firms in the market

Every item on those lists changes the whole curve — a new relationship between price and quantity at every price. The one thing that shifts neither curve is the product's own price: that only moves you along the curve. Next page makes that distinction stick.

WORDS THAT EARN MARKS · 词汇

Increase ≠ extension

Examiners test this every year: a shift is not a movement.

INCREASE / DECREASE — the curve moves

An increase in demand = the whole demand curve shifts right. An increase in supply = the whole supply curve shifts right.

Trigger: a condition changed — income, tastes, costs, tax…

EXTENSION / CONTRACTION — you move along it

An extension = the price rose → a larger quantity. A contraction = the price fell → a smaller quantity.

Trigger: the product's own price changed — nothing else.

Put them together: an increase in demand raises the price, which causes an extension of supply. One sentence — and it is worth marks.

Blue arrow = the curve shifts (an increase in demand) · green arrow = movement along the curve (an extension of supply)

DOUBLE SHIFTS · 总结

Both curves at once — the four cases

Two changes at the same time? Read your case off this table: one of the two answers is certain, the other one is not.
DemandSupply PriceQuantity
↑ rises  D →↑ rises  S → ______
↑ rises  D →↓ falls  ← S ______
↓ falls  ← D↑ rises  S → ______
↓ falls  ← D↓ falls  ← S ______

Why? The two shifts push the same variable in opposite ways. Rows 1 and 4 move the same way, so their quantity effects add up and quantity is the certain one — while the two price effects fight. Rows 2 and 3 move in opposite ways, so their price effects add up and price is the certain one.

The sentence worth marks: same-way shifts → quantity is certain; opposite-way shifts → price is certain. And if the two shifts are the same size they cancel exactly — equal same-way shifts leave the price at ¥30, equal opposite shifts leave the quantity at 5 cups.
