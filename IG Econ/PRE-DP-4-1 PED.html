THE PRICE WAR · 价格战

Cut the price — get richer?

Two real price wars, one milk-tea shop, the same ¥10 cut — with opposite results.

Last summer

Price was ¥40. A rival opened next door, so the shop cut ¥10 → ¥30.

This winter

Business is slow, so the shop cuts ¥10 again → ¥20. Same move as in summer.

Same shop, same ¥10 cut — but the two cuts did not do the same thing to the shop's money. That gap is what today measures.

Summer: ¥40 → ¥30. Sales 3 → 5 cups. Money in: ¥120 → ¥150. Revenue ROSE ✅

Winter: ¥30 → ¥20. Sales 5 → 7 cups. Money in: ¥150 → ¥140. Revenue FELL ❌

"Money in" = price × cups sold. Both cuts sold more cups — that alone decides nothing.

DEFINITION · 定义

Meet PED

We need a number that says how strongly buyers react to a price change. Here it is.

1 · The idea — price elasticity of demand (PED) measures how responsive the quantity demanded is when the price changes.

2 · The formula

PED= %ΔQd%ΔP = ΔQ ÷ QΔP ÷ P = ΔQΔP × PQ

3 · Read the number. PED is negative (price up → Qd down: the law of demand), so economists focus on its size. A PED of −2 means a 1% price rise cuts Qd by 2%.

4 · Why bother? Because the same price cut can raise or sink a firm's revenue — you saw it in the price war. PED is the number that predicts which one happens, and it guides firms' pricing and government taxes.

CALCULATION · 计算

Work out a PED — step by step

Price falls from $10 to $9, and Qd rises from 200 to 240. Four small steps.

1 · %ΔQd — change ÷ original × 100: (240 − 200) ÷ 200 × 100 = +20%

2 · %ΔP — same method: (9 − 10) ÷ 10 × 100 = −10%

3 · Divide — PED = +20% ÷ (−10%) = −2

4 · Read it. The minus sign is just the law of demand. The size 2: a 1% price change moves Qd by 2% — buyers react strongly.

FIVE DEGREES · 谱系

Five degrees of elasticity

Every PED value lands in one of five zones. Click each zone — the curve redraws to match.

Inelastic (< 1): Qd changes by a smaller percentage than price. Typical of necessities — salt, petrol, medicine. Price and revenue move in the same direction.

CONTRAST · 对比

Same price rise, two very different curves

Both demand curves start at ¥30 / 5 cups. Raise the price and watch how differently Qd responds.

Steep curve → inelastic

Buyers barely move: Qd falls by a smaller % than the price rose. |PED| < 1.

Shallow curve → elastic

Buyers flee: Qd falls by a bigger % than the price rose. |PED| > 1.

Careful: "steep/shallow" only compares curves on the same scale. Always check the axes before judging.

DETERMINANT 1 · 因素

The main factor: availability of substitutes

Coursebook: the availability of substitutes is the main factor.

A close substitute exists → elastic

If a product has a close substitute of similar quality and price, demand is likely to be elastic: a price rise makes consumers switch.

No close substitute → inelastic

With no close substitute, demand will probably be inelastic: quantity demanded will not fall much, as there is no suitable alternative to switch to.

Ask this first — every other influence works through it: more substitutes = more elastic.

DETERMINANT 2 · 因素

Second: the proportion of income spent on it

Coursebook: a small proportion of income → inelastic; a large proportion → elastic.

Salt — a small share of income

¥1 → ¥1.5 is a rise of 50% — but only ¥0.5 more. Barely noticed → demand is inelastic.

A new car — a large share of income

¥1,000,000 → ¥1,500,000 is the same 50% — but ¥500,000 more. Buyers postpone → demand is elastic.

Same 50%, different money — ¥0.5 vs ¥500,000. What matters is the share of income the rise takes.

OTHER INFLUENCES · 因素

Other influences

Five more influences — each still works through substitutes.
  • Necessity or luxury? — Luxuries (gold jewellery) can be left unbought → elastic; necessities (soap) cannot be cut back → inelastic.
  • Addictive or delayable? — Addictive products (cigarettes) are hard to give up → inelastic; a purchase that can be delayed → elastic.
  • Complements — a cheap product used with an expensive one (a phone charger with a smartphone) is a small part of the combined spend → inelastic.
  • The time period — given more time to find alternatives, demand becomes more elastic in the long run than in the short run.
  • How narrowly the market is defined — the narrower the definition, the more substitutes it has → the more elastic (one brand of tea vs tea in general vs hot drinks in general).

PED → REVENUE · 收入

How does TR move when the price falls?

1 · Watch the rectangle

Press Play ▶ — the price slides from top to bottom. Watch only the area.

2 · What did the area do?

It grew, peaked in the middle, then shrank.

3 · Turn it into PED

TR rising → ELASTIC · TR falling → INELASTIC · peak → PED = 1.

4 · Conclusion

Straight line: PED falls as Q rises — elastic, PED = 1 in the middle (Q = 5.5), then inelastic. Not the slope.

P × Q = the rectangle
Q × TR = the area, as a curve
