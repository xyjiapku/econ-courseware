Step 2

Where are these points?

先判断 · 他工作了几小时？

Think first — don't look yet

For each point, ask: what did Robinson get, and how many hours did he work?

A (40, 40) — 40 fish + 40 coconuts → hours?
B (20, 20) — 20 fish + 20 coconuts → hours?
C (60, 40) — 60 fish + 40 coconuts → hours?
Step 2

On · Inside · Outside

用工作小时数判断

A (40, 40) · On

4 h + 4 h = 8 hours ✓ all time used

B (20, 20) · Inside

2 h + 2 h = 4 hours — labour time not fully used!

C (60, 40) · Outside

6 h + 4 h = 10 hours — impossible, he works at most 8 h

Key idea: on = full use · inside = waste · outside = impossible
Step 3

Slope = Opportunity Cost

沿 PPC 移动：斜率就是机会成本

Move along the line

From A (10, 70) to B (40, 40): + 30 fish → − 30 coconuts

Opportunity cost of 30 fish = 30 coconuts
= 1 coconut per fish (constant)
Slope = ΔCoconuts / ΔFish = 30 / 30 = 1
👉 The slope of the PPC is the opportunity cost

Tips

Drawing the PPC — Common Mistakes

画图常见错误

✅ Do this

  • Label both axes — Fish and Coconuts
  • Shape — bowed out (concave to origin) = increasing OC
  • Ends touch the axes — the curve forms a closed region with them

    • ❌ Avoid these

  • Missing axis labels
  • Wrong shape — bowed in (convex) = decreasing OC, not increasing
  • Curve stops in mid-air — not closed with the axes
    • IGCSE exam tip: numbers on the axes are NOT required — labels, shape (concave = increasing OC) and closure are what count.

    Summary

    Key Ideas

    本课要点

    What is a PPC?

    Maximum combinations of two goods an economy can produce with all resources fully used — from Robinson's table to the graph.

    Three key ideas

    ① Straight PPC → constant OC;  bowed PPC → increasing OC
    ② Slope = opportunity cost
    ③ On = full use · Inside = waste · Outside = impossible

    Drawing checklist

    Label axes ✓ · Ends touch axes ✓ · Bowed out (concave) ✓

    Remember: to produce more of one good, you give up another — that is opportunity cost, and the PPC shows it.
