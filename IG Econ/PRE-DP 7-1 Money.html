Adam Smith’s guess

BEFORE MONEY
In 1776 he asked how people traded before money — and answered: by barter.

“When the division of labour first began, this power of exchanging must have been very much clogged… one man wants to buy, and another wants to sell, and they cannot.”

His picture: goods straight for goods — the fish to whoever had shoes.

The catch: that needs a perfect match.

His fix: money — what it does, from the next page.

Medium of exchange

FUNCTION 1 OF 4
DEFINITION

generally acceptable for buying and selling.

One thing everybody takes — so the trade splits into two easy halves.

Under barter one person must want your fish and have shoes. With money you sell to anyone, then buy from anyone else.

Without it, trade stops at your neighbours.

IN PRACTICE · A shovel was the tool everyone used and wore out fast, so everyone would take one — and Chinese bu coins kept its shape long after the tool was gone.

Unit of account

FUNCTION 2 OF 4
DEFINITION

it places a value on an item so prices can be expressed and compared.

One measuring rod — instead of a price for every pair of goods.

Under barter every price must be quoted in every other good: 10 goods give 45 ratios, 100 goods give 4,950.

Without it buyers cannot compare, and a firm cannot add up what it owns.

Store of value

FUNCTION 3 OF 4
DEFINITION

it can be saved and remain acceptable for spending later.

Value you can carry forward — earn now, spend later.

Income arrives in lumps; spending is continuous — money bridges the gap. Without it, everyone spends at once or hoards goods that decay.

Standard for deferred payment

FUNCTION 4 OF 4
DEFINITION

it allows people to borrow now and repay later.

A debt written in money — borrow today, repay the same number later.

Lending needs a unit that keeps its meaning. Borrow three sacks of rice and repay three sacks: if the harvest doubles, the loan changed without anyone agreeing to it.

Credit disappears at once.

IN PRACTICE · A ¥3,000 loan repaid as ¥3,300 next year — both sums fixed at the start.

Money has a history

Each form solved a problem the last one could not — step through them.
before coins

Shells, salt, cloth. Accepted, but impossible to cut into change.

c.600 BC · Lydia

A stamp on weighed metal. Portable and homogeneous at last.

Song China · jiaozi

Paper for large sums. Light to carry, but it needs trust.

today

Notes, cards, phones. Fast and cheap — and almost no metal at all.

1 / 4

Every new form wins on one attribute and trades away another.

A coin is a promise about metal

Weighing silver and trusting a stamp are two different things.

Metal as money: value = weight × purity — every trade needs a scale.

Metal as a coin: value = face value — the mint guarantees the metal.

The risk: clip the edges and the stamp becomes a lie.

Gresham: bad money drives out good.

Newton and the recoinage

1696: silver worth less than its coin.

The problem. People clipped the edges — the metal was worth having.

The result. Full-weight coins were hoarded; clipped ones stayed.

The fix. Re-mint at full weight with milled edges.

So: no intrinsic value is a feature.

Functions are what money does; characteristics are what an item must be

When supply is not limited

Print enough of it and money stops being money.
Germany 1921–24

Notes printed to pay reparations; prices re-set daily.

China 1948–49

The gold yuan lost its value within months.

Zimbabwe 2008

A 100 trillion dollar note bought a loaf.

Venezuela 2018–21

Banknotes sold by weight, not by count.

1 / 4

In all four the quantity of money exploded; what collapsed first was acceptability.

Forms of money today

Three ways to hold it — and one legal idea that is not a fourth form.
Coins

Small purchases and change. Legal tender up to a stated value.

Banknotes

Larger purchases; the bulk of cash. Legal tender without limit.

Bank accounts

The largest form by value; moved by card or phone.

But only cash is legal tender

Legal tender — any form of payment which, by law, must be accepted in settlement of a debt. A shop may refuse a card; it cannot refuse a note.

Digital currency

Money that exists only in electronic form — the one thing that makes CBDC different.
Already digital: a bank account

Your balance is a claim on a bank. If the bank fails, you queue with everyone else.

New: central bank digital currency

A claim on the central bank. No bank to fail; cheaper and open to the unbanked.

The only question that matters: who owes you the money — a bank, or the state? Both can be hacked.

Virtual currency

EXTENSION

Bitcoin is not money yet. It is a financial asset — valuable, tradable, and priced in money.

Cryptocurrency — online only, over 10,000 of them; held for investment, and its value swings sharply.

Seven tests: limited ✓ divisible ✓ portable ✓

But acceptable ✗ and stable in value ✗ — so it works as an asset, not as money.

Smith was half right

Extension · exchange is ancient, but barter was not how most people traded.

Exchange is very old. Obsidian and salt moved hundreds of kilometres before coins.

But not by barter. Traditional societies ran on reciprocity — gifts, sharing, credit.

Money made exchange impersonal; that is what changed.
