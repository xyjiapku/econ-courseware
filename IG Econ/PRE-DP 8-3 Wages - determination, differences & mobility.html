A wage is a price

The wage is the price of labour; the wage rate is that payment per unit of time — per hour, day or week.

Same rules as any market

1 Workers supply labour; firms demand it.
2 The crossing point gives the equilibrium wage We.
3 No shortage, no surplus: the market clears.
Everything in this lesson is this one diagram, moved.
A wage is a price, not a reward for effort.

Demand for labour — and what shifts it

Demand for labour is derived: firms hire only because consumers buy the product.

D → D1: demand rises

wage ↑ · employment ↑

Three shifters

1 demand for the product
2 productivity of workers
3 price of machines
A higher wage on the same curve is a movement; these three shift the curve.

Jevons paradox: productivity does not kill jobs

When technology makes a resource more efficient, total use of it can rise, not fall.
Coal. Watt's steam engine used coal more efficiently. Coal grew cheaper, engines spread to every factory, mine and ship — and total coal burned soared.
The machine gun. One gunner could do the work of a rank of riflemen. Armies did not shrink: wars grew, and factories needed more hands for guns and shells.
✗ Common answer
Productivity ↑ → each worker makes more → fewer workers needed → D shifts left.
✓ What actually happens
Unit cost ↓ → price ↓ → demand for the product ↑↑ → derived demand for labour shifts right.
That is why productivity was listed on the previous page as a cause of demand rising.

Supply of labour — and what shifts it

Supply of labour = workers willing and able to work at each wage rate.

S → S1: supply rises

wage ↓ · employment ↑

Four shifters

1 population / migration
2 qualifications required
3 non-wage appeal
4 wages in other jobs
A certificate does not raise pay directly — it shrinks the supply of people who can do the job.

Trade unions

A trade union protects and improves members’ pay and conditions through collective bargaining.

Read it

Union wage Wf sits above We.
At Wf, Qd < Qs → a surplus of labour.
Insiders keep the job;
outsiders queue.
Strongest where membership is high and workers are hard to replace.
Higher pay for members can mean fewer jobs in total.

The minimum wage: short run vs long run

A minimum wage is a legal minimum hourly rate that employers must pay.

The long-run adjustment

1 Short run: Wf above We → Qd < Qs, a surplus appears.
2 Long run: higher pay lifts motivation and productivity, so demand shifts D → D′.
3 At Wf the two curves can meet again — the surplus disappears.
Card & Krueger, New Jersey 1992 vs Pennsylvania: fast-food jobs did not fall. Card shared the 2021 Nobel.
Exam answer is conditional: a large rise, with elastic labour demand, still costs jobs.

Why wages differ: three layers

A wage is set in a market — but the labour market is not an ordinary one.
01
Demand & supply
The starting point: the wage is a price. Scarce skills, long training and productivity shift D and S.
Always explain this layer first.
02
Bargaining power
A single worker faces a firm with the stronger hand. A trade union pools workers and lifts their power, so pay can rise above We.
This is what makes labour special.
03
Government
Minimum wage, public-sector pay scales, equal-pay law — rules that set or limit pay directly.
Add this layer last.
The layers stack: start with D/S, then ask who can bargain, then ask what the law requires.

Gap 1 · Skill

Long training keeps the supply of skilled labour small — so the wage is high.

Why the gap appears

1
Twelve years of training → few qualified workers → S shifts left.
2
Their output is worth more → D shifts right.
3
Both push the wage up: scarcity, not effort.

Read it on the model

Surgeon vs cleaner: a small supply meets a high demand.
The same two curves explain every skill gap — no new diagram is needed.
Exam chain: difference in training → effect on S or D → direction of the wage.

Gap 2 · Gender, discrimination — and how big is it?

Discrimination = paying equally productive workers differently because of who they are, not what they produce.

Three things often mixed up

1 Real differences — hours, experience, career breaks.
2 Discrimination — same productivity, lower pay.
3 Policy — equal-pay law; transparent scales.
Explain it in words — the syllabus requires no diagram.
It belongs to the bargaining layer, not a shift in D or S.

Measuring the gap

Numerator = higher pay − lower pay
Denominator = the higher pay (the base)
40 & 34 → 15%
Using 34 as the base gives 17.6% — always state the base.
12,000 vs 9,000 → ?
40,000 vs 3,500 → ?
An average gap alone never proves discrimination.

Dull and dangerous jobs pay a premium

A compensating wage differential is extra pay that offsets a job’s unpleasant features.

How the premium arises

1
Narrow job — one repeated task, little variety.
2
Fewer takers — risk, dirt or monotony drives applicants away; supply shifts left.
3
Higher pay — the employer must pay a premium to fill the post.

Read it on the model

Risk, dirt or monotony → fewer takers → supply shifts left → the wage rises.
D unchanged · S shifts left
Demand for the job has not moved. The premium is the price of finding anyone willing to do it.
Exam chain: unpleasant feature → effect on S → direction of the wage.
Recall 3.3a: the miner and the mercenary are paid for what the civil servant and teacher are not.

Labour mobility: two kinds, two sets of causes

Occupational mobility changes job; geographical mobility changes place.

Raises mobility

retraining · recognised qualifications · cheaper transport · affordable housing · job information

Blocks mobility

long training · family ties · language · house prices · immigration rules
Better mobility lets supply reach the jobs — so regional wage gaps narrow.

What mobility changes

Three groups, one mechanism: supply becomes more responsive.
WhoGains from higher mobilityCost of low mobility
Workerscan move to better-paid jobs; more choicetrapped in a declining industry
Firmsfill vacancies faster; wider skill poolskill shortages; must raise wages

Structural unemployment = unemployment caused by a mismatch between workers’ skills or location and the jobs available — it persists even when vacancies exist.
High mobility does not remove wage differences — it stops the same gap from persisting.
