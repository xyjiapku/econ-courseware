How a law is born · Two laws, one logic

Side by side — physics and economics

Two different sciences. One identical recipe. That's why the lines and the predictions look the same.

🔬 Hooke's Law

① Control: same spring

② Change: force F

③ Record: stretch x

④ Fit: F = kx · slope = k (stiffness)

Same shape, every time you stretch a spring.

📈 Law of Supply

① Control: ceteris paribus

② Change: price P

③ Record: quantity Qs

④ Fit: Qs ∝ P · slope = 5 (sensitivity)

Same shape, every time the canteen raises a price.

Different science, same logic. From clean data to a straight line to a law — this is how knowledge is built.

Scientific method · Hypothesis, evidence & P vs N

Hypotheses must be testable — and tested by evidence

A hypothesis is an if…then… prediction. It must be falsifiable — data can prove it wrong.

✓ Testable hypothesis

"If milk tea price rises, then cups sold fall."
→ Check receipts. Data can confirm or refute it.

✗ Not testable

"The government should cut taxes."
→ No data can test a value judgment.

Evidence decides — if data matches the prediction, we tentatively accept; if not, we reject or refine.

Positive — what IS

"Unemployment is 5%." · "Sales doubled after BOGO."

Normative — what SHOULD BE

"Govt SHOULD cut taxes." · "Canteen SHOULD keep deal."

Policy debates are mostly normative — but good policy needs positive analysis first.
Scientific method · Step ③ Assume — ceteris paribus

The most important assumption in economics

Ceteris paribus = Latin for "other things equal"
Scientific method · Step ③ in the real world

How do economists control the "other things"?

In the real world, "other things" never stay equal. So economists design comparisons that make them almost equal — a clean approximation of ceteris paribus.

🔬 Randomised experiment

Split students randomly: one group gets a milk-tea discount, the other doesn't. The only difference between the groups is the "treatment".

🎲 Natural experiment

The government suddenly taxes sugary drinks. Compare sales just before and just after — everything else is roughly unchanged.

⏱️ Before / after with a control

Compare the change in canteen sales across exam weeks for A class (exams) vs B class (no exams). Subtracting the control's change removes the common trend.

📐 Statistical control

Feed data into a regression that "holds income, weather and term time fixed" — the coefficient on price then shows its own effect.

Every study is an approximation of ceteris paribus — never perfect. The craft of economics is making the comparison as clean as possible.

Models · Real-world complexity

Real-world models can be very complex

Our canteen model has two curves. A central bank's model runs hundreds of equations — but it is still a map, not the economy itself.

🧩 Hundreds of variables

GDP, inflation, unemployment, exchange rates, interest rates, house prices…

🔁 Feedback loops

Wages → spending → demand → jobs → wages — every link loops back.

🌐 Open economy

Exports, imports and capital flows respond to every policy change.

💻 Solved by supercomputers

DSGE models (dynamic stochastic general equilibrium) run thousands of "what-if" scenarios.

Why learn simple models first? Simple models build intuition; complex models make predictions. You need the map before the territory.
Disagreement · Why economists disagree

Why do economists disagree?

🧪 No lab experiments

We can't hold the economy still in a laboratory.

🌀 Many variables at once

Isolating cause and effect in the real world is hard.

📊 Data limitations

Incomplete, unreliable, or unmeasurable variables.

Same hypothesis, different data → conflicting results.

⚖️ Value judgments — a different kind of disagreement

Even with the same facts, economists may back different policies because their values differ. Example — "Everyone pays ¥16" is equality: measurable, a positive claim. "Students should get a discount" is equity: normative, and people disagree about what is fair.

Disagreement · Two economists, two styles

Two economists on how economists think

John Maynard Keynes

1883–1946 · British economist

"The ideas of economists and political philosophers, both when they are right and when they are wrong, are more powerful than is commonly understood. Indeed the world is ruled by little else. Practical men, who believe themselves to be quite exempt from any intellectual influences, are usually the slaves of some defunct economist."

— politicians are often slaves to economic ideas they don't even know they're following.

林毅夫 Justin Yifu Lin

1952– · Chinese economist · World Bank Chief Economist (2008–2012)

"我的政策建议主要是'敲边鼓'——提出方向性的建议，具体细节留给决策者根据实际情况去判断。经济学家的角色是提供分析框架和逻辑，而不是替决策者做决定。"

— the economist's role is to provide frameworks, not to dictate policy.

Both agree: economics is a way of thinking, not a set of doctrines. The best economists combine rigorous analysis with intellectual humility.
