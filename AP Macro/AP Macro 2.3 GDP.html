Aim 1 — economic growth — is the one we need a number for. That number is GDP, and most of today is about how it is built.
Aim 1 · Economic growth, drawn two ways

Short run: AD shifts. Long run: capacity itself shifts.

Short-run (actual) growth
Long-run (potential) growth
Actual growth moves output towards existing capacity — AD↗ → Y rises. Potential growth moves capacity itself — with AD and SRAS unchanged, only LRAS shifts right, so potential output rises.
Aim 1 · Why aim for it?

Growth is a means, not an end

Why aim for it? Raises living standards, improves tax revenue, and creates jobs.

① Raises living standards

More output per person — more goods and services to go around.

② Improves tax revenue

Higher incomes and spending widen the tax base without raising rates.

③ Creates jobs

Expanding output needs more workers — the flip side of aim 2.

The other side · What is a recession?

When growth goes into reverse

Key definition: A recession occurs when real GDP declines over a period of six months (two successive quarters) or more.

Two successive quarters

Not one bad quarter — the rule needs two in a row.

Note the word "real"

Output must fall after inflation is stripped out. We cannot tell growth from inflation until Lesson 2.4.

National income accounting

The definition, and why every word matters

Economists collect statistics on production, income, investment, and savings. The most important measure of growth is GDP.

Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is the dollar value of all final goods and services produced within a country in one year.
National income accounting · The phrase that does the most work

"All final goods and services"

GDP is the dollar value of all final goods and services produced within a country in one year.

Final

Sold to the final user — not resold, not built into something else. Counting anything else double counts.

Not intermediate

The steel in a car, the flour in bread — their value is already inside the final price.

Not just goods

Services count too — haircuts, health care, insurance. In the U.S. they are about two-thirds of consumption.

If it has no market price, it is not here. That single rule explains most of the limitations page.
Three ways of calculating GDP ① · Income approach

Add up every income the production created

Income approach: adds up all the income earned from producing goods and services.
Labour → wages

Income earned by workers.

Land → rent

Income earned by landowners.

Capital → interest

Income earned by capital.

Entrepreneurship → profit

The residual reward to the risk-taker.

收入法 — China's NBS wording 中国国家统计局口径

增加值 ＝ 劳动者报酬 ＋ 生产税净额 ＋ 固定资产折旧 ＋ 营业盈余

—— 国家统计局《中国 GDP 年度核算说明》

Same logic, different institutional detail: wages + rent + interest + profit (AP) vs. 劳动者报酬 + 生产税净额 + 折旧 + 营业盈余 (China).

"I" · What counts — and the part nobody thinks about

Investment is not what your bank calls investment

GDP (Y) = C + I + G + (X − M)

NEVER

Investment is never when individuals buy assets like stocks and bonds — a financial claim changing hands, not new output.

ALWAYS

Investment is always when businesses buy capital like machines, resources, and tools.

Inventories 存货

Goods produced and held in storage in anticipation of later sales.

Counted in the year they are produced (as Investment), not the year they are sold.
Two words, one letter: in macro I means real capital. Buying shares is saving. Unsold stock is still this year's output.
"G" · What government spending counts — and what doesn't

Government expenditures

GDP (Y) = C + I + G + (X − M)

Counted

Payments made by the government for goods and services — a fighter jet, a teacher's salary, a road.

Not counted ①

Money spent on transfer payments like welfare, social security, and subsidies — nothing produced in exchange.

Not counted ②

It also does not include interest payments on the national debt.

The test is always the same: did the government receive newly produced goods or services this year? If not, it is a transfer.
Three ways of calculating GDP ③

Output / Value-added approach

GDP (Y) = C + I + G + (X − M)
Output / Value-added approach: add up the dollar value added at each stage of the production process.

Value added =

Sales − Purchased inputs

Each firm keeps only the value it created.

Why bother?

When you cannot observe the final price — or want to see where in the chain value is created — this is the only route.

Sum of value added across all stages always equals the final good's price. Same number, third door.

Imputed rental value 虚拟租金

You own your home. GDP still counts rent.

What & Why

  • Non-market service from owner-occupied dwellings; no cash rent is paid.
  • 5–10% of GDP in advanced economies (≈9% in US, ≈8% in EU).
  • Must be imputed to keep GDP neutral between owning and renting (SNA 2008).

How to estimate

Comparable-market-rent method — value the home at the rent a similar property would fetch.

三法对照

收入法：房东的租金收入
支出法：房东自住的租金支出
生产法：房屋产生的居住服务

The tension worth naming: GDP wants a market transaction — yet here statisticians invent one. That inconsistency is the limitations page in one line.
Three ways of calculating GDP · The punchline

Each method should generate the same number

GDP (Y) = C + I + G + (X − M)
Each of these methods should generate the same number.

Income approach

Adds up all the income earned from producing — wages, rent, interest, profit.

Expenditure approach

Adds up all spending on final goods — C + I + G + (X − M).

Value-added approach

Adds the dollar value added at each stage of production.

Why: one transaction, three viewpoints. What is spent is what is earned is what is added.
What GDP misses · Limitations of GDP

GDP measures PRODUCTION. It is NOT a measure of well-being.

EXCLUDED — or GDP would be OVERSTATED

  • Intermediate goods — already in the final price
  • Used / secondhand goods — counted when produced
  • Stocks & bonds — no new production
  • Transfer payments — nothing produced in exchange
  • Imports — not produced domestically

MISSED — so GDP is UNDERSTATED

  • Barter — no market transaction
  • Home production — housework, family care
  • Underground economy — unreported & illegal

(A market transaction is required to value output.)

GDP ≠ Quality of Life

Same GDP per capita, very different lives.

Not captured by GDP at all:

  • Income distribution — output may go to a few
  • Leisure — longer hours, less vacation
  • Congestion, crime, safety, social stress, inequality

To compare living standards

GDP per capita = GDP ÷ Population

Alternatives

Genuine Progress Indicator, happiness indexes — none has replaced GDP.

Summary · GDP in five lines

What to walk out with

① The definition

Market value of all final goods & services produced within a country in a year — every word is a test question.

② No double counting

Count the final good, or count value added at each stage — never both, never all sales.

③ Three approaches, one number

C + I + G + (X − M) = wages + rent + interest + profit = sum of value added.

④ It has blind spots

Excludes intermediate, used, financial, transfers, imports; misses nonmarket and underground activity.

⑤ It isn't well-being

Use GDP per capita for comparisons — and remember what it leaves out.
