Unit 4 · Lesson 7 · Why look beyond cash

Cash is convenient — and that is all it is

Last lesson's balance

Holding money costs you the interest you could have earned. The heavier the interest pan, the less cash you want to hold.

So what is on the other pan?

Everything else you could own — each one a financial asset, a claim on future money:

deposits 存款
bonds 债券
stocks 股票
funds 基金

And each one is a trade-off

More return, less safety or less liquidity. Choosing between them is the whole subject of this lesson.

A financial asset is simply a claim on someone else's future payment.

Unit 4 · Lesson 7 · The three attributes

Liquidity, return, risk — you cannot have all three

Liquidity

How fast you can turn it into money without losing value.

Rate of return

Interest, dividends and price gains — as a percentage per year.

Risk

Extra return is the payment for extra risk. So the top-right quadrant is full of assets — and the top-left stays empty.

Read the four quadrants: high risk can pay well or go badly; low risk never pays well. Yields are September 2026; stock and T-bill figures are long-run averages.
Unit 4 · Lesson 7 · Financial literacy ①

Compounding: slow, then explosive

FV = PV × (1 + r)n

What the curve says

Interest earns interest. Most of the growth arrives in the last years — which is why starting early matters more than adding more.

After10 yr20 yr30 yr
$1,000 at 7%$1,967$3,870$7,612

The same curve, reversed

Debt compounds too — and so does inflation. The shape is identical; only the sign changes.

Time beats size: the last ten years add more than the first twenty.
Unit 4 · Lesson 7 · Financial literacy ②

The Rule of 72, the Rule of 70 — and the exact answer

Growth rate 7.0% a year
Rule of 72
72 ÷ r
10.3 yr
Rule of 70
70 ÷ r
10.0 yr
Exact
ln2 ÷ ln(1+r)
10.2 yr
At 7%: exact 10.2 yr — the rule of 72 is closer here.

Which rule, when?

Below ~5% the rule of 70 is closer (69.3 is exact for continuous growth); above 5%, 72 wins.

Why 72 and 70 anyway?

72 divides neatly by 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 12; 70 is easy in tens. Both are mental-arithmetic tools.

Use it on inflation

At 2%, prices take 36 years to double; at 3.90%, money doubles in ~18.

中文速记：翻倍年数 ≈ 72 ÷ 利率（%）

Unit 4 · Lesson 7 · Government vs corporate

Same promise, different risk

US Treasuries

The safest bonds in the world: bills (under a year), notes (2–10 years), bonds (20–30 years). The 10-year yield is the world's benchmark — about 5.2% in September 2026.

Why the Fed cares

This market is huge and liquid, so it is where the Fed buys and sells to move the money supply (4.6).

Corporate bonds

A company can go bankrupt, so it must pay more: roughly a percentage point or two above Treasuries for solid firms, far more for shaky ones.

Junk bonds

Low-rated issuers pay the highest yields — and default most often. The extra yield is the price of that risk.

No free lunch: the extra coupon is what the market charges for the chance of not being repaid.
Unit 4 · Lesson 7 · Real world: how a local government borrows

Why borrow through a company?

A local government’s own borrowing is capped and must sit in its budget. So to build more, it creates a company — and the company borrows.
① The debt is the company’s, not the government’s.
That is borrowing in disguise — 变相发债.
② But everyone assumed the government would repay it anyway.
That assumption has a name: an implicit guarantee — 隐性担保.
And the market priced that assumption (Feb 2025):
A province’s own bond 20 bp
An LGFV 98 bp — about 5×, for the same money

Unit 4 · Lesson 7 · Equity

Stocks: owning a slice, not lending

A different claim

A bondholder is a lender; a shareholder is an owner — paid last, after everyone else, and with no fixed promise.

Where the return comes from

Dividends plus price gains. Historically US large-company stocks averaged about 10% a year — the top of the risk ladder.

And the price today

The S&P 500 stood near 7,700 in September 2026. You can own the whole index for about 0.03% a year in fees.

The New York Stock Exchange trading floor
Highest expected return, highest risk — and the only asset on the menu that makes you an owner.

Unit 4 · Lesson 7 · Before you move on

Three ways students lose points

1
Promising high return with low risk
There is no such asset. Risk is what the extra return buys — and the exam tests exactly this trade-off.
2
Confusing liquidity with safety
Cash is the most liquid asset and the worst long-run store of value. Liquid and safe are different words.
3
Reading the coupon as the return
A bond's coupon is fixed; its price and yield move with interest rates — that is 4.9.
All three come down to one sentence: every asset is a bundle of liquidity, return and risk.
