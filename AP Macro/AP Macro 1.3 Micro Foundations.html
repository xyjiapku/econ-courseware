Demand ④ · From D to AD

AD vs. D — same direction, different reasons

Aggregate Demand (AD) = total spending in the whole economy: C + I + G + NX.

AD falls when the price level rises — but NOT because of substitution. There is no "other economy" to switch to!
AD = C + I + G + NX at each price level

Supply ③ · Short-run vs. long-run aggregate supply

SRAS slopes up; LRAS is vertical

LRAS — vertical: in the long run, prices and wages are fully flexible. A change in PL only changes prices — output returns to potential GDP*. LRAS marks the natural rate of output.
SRAS — slopes up: in the short run, some prices/wages are sticky. A higher PL cuts real wages → firms cut production. Hence SRAS slopes upward.
SRAS + LRAS = the supply side of AD–AS (Unit 3). Shocks shift SRAS; only real shocks shift LRAS.
All three meet at E (PL*, GDP*) — long-run equilibrium

  1. ① A new study finds that coffee has health benefits. (coffee market)
    D → → P* ▲, Q* ▲
  2. ② The price of coffee beans rises sharply. (coffee market)
    S ← (input cost ▲) → P* ▲, Q* ▼

    D → AND S → → Q* ▲ definitely, P* ? (depends on relative size)

    Per-unit cost ▲ → S ← → P* ▲, Q* ▼

Toolbox ② · Marginal thinking

Marginal = the change (Δ) — the next unit, the extra dollar

"Marginal" just means the next one — the change from "one more". Always ask: what happens when we add one more unit, one more dollar, one more step?

Total

the whole — all dollars, all units, all of something

e.g. total consumption C = 80

→

Marginal

the next one — the change, the increment, the delta (Δ)

e.g. ΔC = 6 when Y goes up by 10

Two ways to read a marginal number: as a difference (Y went 100 → 110, C went 80 → 86, so ΔC/ΔY = 6/10 = 0.6) — or as a slope on a graph. Same idea, different view.
Next: we use marginal thinking to look at MPC and MPS — the marginal propensities to consume and save.
Toolbox ③ · Marginal propensities

MPC vs. MPS — where income goes

From each extra dollar of income, households consume part and save part:

APC = C / Y

Average propensity to consume — total consumption ÷ total income

APS = S / Y

Average propensity to save — total saving ÷ total income

First the averages — then the marginals.
MPC = slope of the consumption line · MPS = slope of the saving line
