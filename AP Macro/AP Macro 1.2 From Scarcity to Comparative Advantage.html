Concept ③ · The PPC and its points

PPC = maximum combinations of two goods an economy can produce with available resources & technology.

PPC — three kinds of points

A — on the curve

Efficient — full employment of resources

B — inside the curve

Inefficient — unused or misallocated resources

C — outside the curve

Unattainable — beyond current capacity

Macro lens: outside the PPC is unattainable in the long run — but short-run output can temporarily exceed potential (overtime). Macro calls this an inflationary gap — the economy "overheats".

Exam traps

Four ways students lose points

① AA ≠ CA

Confusing absolute & comparative advantage — always compare opportunity costs, not just output.

② OC upside-down

Output table: OC = other ÷ this. Input table: OC = this ÷ other.

③ Same-good CA for both

Impossible — if A has lower OC in X, B must have lower OC in Y.

④ Terms outside range

If the ratio isn't between both OCs, one side simply won't trade.

Summary

Key takeaways

① Scarcity = unlimited wants vs. limited resources.

② Opportunity cost = the next-best alternative foregone.

③ PPC: on = efficient · inside = inefficient · outside = unattainable.

④ Bowed-out PPC = increasing OC · straight = constant OC.

⑤ Comparative advantage = lower OC → trade between OCs → consume beyond PPC.

In macro, the PPC is drawn with consumer goods vs capital goods — bowed outward.
