Toolbox ④ · The circular flow

One person's spending is another person's income

The government spends $100 repaving a road. The money does not stop there: every dollar spent is a dollar of income for somebody else — and income is exactly what we count as GDP.
Every ripple = MPC × the one before it

Policy ② · Taxes work through spending

The tax multiplier

Tax multiplier = − MPC / (1 − MPC) = − MPC / MPS
A tax cut raises disposable income, but households save part of it first — only MPC of it becomes spending. With MPC = 0.6: − 0.6/0.4 = −1.5.
Policy (MPC = 0.6)MultiplierΔAD
ΔG = +$10 bn×2.5+$25 bn
ΔT = −$10 bn×1.5+$15 bn
Same direction, smaller punch: |tax multiplier| = spending multiplier − 1.

Policy ③ · Think deeper

Tax more, spend it all — does GDP still rise?

The tax multiplier is smaller than the spending multiplier. So: raise taxes by $10 bn, spend the whole $10 bn, keep the budget balanced — is that still expansionary?
+25 from spending, −15 from taxes: net +10
Policy ④ · The two levers inside the model

Fiscal policy in the AD-AS model

Fiscal policy = the government's use of spending (G) and taxation (T) to shift AD towards potential output GDP*.

The size of the AD shift is not the size of the policy: ΔAD = ΔSpending × multiplier (or ΔT × tax multiplier).

Policy ⑤ · Built in, no vote needed

Automatic stabilizers

Features of the budget that dampen the cycle automatically — no new legislation required.

Progressive income taxes

Boom: incomes ↑ → tax revenue ↑ → spending dampened. Recession: revenue ↓ → spending cushioned.

Unemployment benefits

Means-tested transfers rise automatically in a downturn, supporting incomes and spending.

Automatic vs discretionary

Discretionary policy needs legislation and suffers lags; automatic stabilizers act immediately, every cycle.

Automatic stabilizers flatten the swings around the trend
Policy ⑥ · Procyclical & countercyclical

With the cycle, or against it?

A variable or a policy is procyclical when it moves in the same direction as the cycle — it amplifies the swing. It is countercyclical when it moves in the opposite direction — it damps the swing.

Procyclical — amplifies

Moves with GDP: up in a boom, down in a slump.

Investment, hiring, bank lending, consumer durables … and, politically, spending cuts forced in a slump.

Countercyclical — damps

Moves against GDP: up in a slump, down in a boom.

Automatic stabilizers — progressive taxes, unemployment benefits — and discretionary expansion in a slump.

Automatic stabilizers are countercyclical by design. That is precisely why they are called stabilizers.
In phase, wider swings → amplifies
Out of phase → offsets the swing
