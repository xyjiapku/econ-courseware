Unit 4 · Lesson 2 · Measuring money

M1 — the medium-of-exchange measure

The money measures are drawn by function. M1 collects everything that does the spending job — assets you can pay with directly.

One narrower name still: M0 = currency in circulation only — the cash inside M1.

中文速记：M0 = 流通中的现金（纸币 + 硬币）；M1 = M0 + 活期存款 —— 能直接用来支付的钱。

M1 is the narrowest spendable measure. The US M1 total in August 2026 was about $20.0 trillion.
Unit 4 · Lesson 2 · The wider measures

M2 — M1 plus the store-of-value measure

中文速记：M1 = 现金 + 活期存款；M2 = M1 + 储蓄存款、小额定期（CD）、货币市场基金；M0 = 流通中的现金；MB（基础货币）= M0 + 银行准备金。

M2 = M1 + near monies: $23.3 trillion in August 2026. One function, two measures.
MB = currency + bank reserves — the banks' money, outside M1 and M2.

Unit 4 · Lesson 2 · Beyond the exam · Crypto

Bitcoin: where it came from

Satoshi Nakamoto — a pseudonym; the face is unknown

2008: a nine-page paper

A nine-page paper by Satoshi Nakamoto — a name nobody has ever identified. The first block was mined in January 2009.

A bitcoin token — the real thing is a ledger entry

The blockchain: a shared ledger

Money as a record — in software: a public ledger copied to thousands of computers, new blocks about every ten minutes.

Hard rules, written in code: supply capped at 21 million coins; new coins created by "mining", reward halving about every four years.
For the first time, the village ledger runs without the village — no bank, no government, nobody in charge.

Unit 4 · Lesson 2 · Beyond the exam · The verdict

Run Bitcoin through the definition

TestResultWhy
Medium of exchangeWeakVery few merchants accept it; settlement is slow and costly.
Unit of accountFailsAlmost nothing is priced in BTC — wages, rent and menus are in dollars.
Store of valueFailsYear-end closes ran from $320 to $93,429 over thirteen years.
Widely acceptedFailsAcceptance is the one property it cannot borrow from the others.

On our properties list it actually scores well: divisible, durable, portable and strictly limited. It still is not money.

By the definition

Not money. It is not generally accepted as a means of payment, and it appears in neither M1 nor M2.

What it actually is

A speculative digital asset — closer to a commodity than to a currency.

Answer the exam question with the definition, not the hype: acceptance is the test, and Bitcoin fails it.
Unit 4 · Lesson 2 · Beyond the exam · Digital money

Private tokens vs central-bank money

Private digital money — no issuer, no peg. The market sets the price.
Stablecoins promise 1 token = $1, backed by reserves.

Central bank digital currency

Issued by the central bank itself, not by a company. The digital yuan (e-CNY) is live; a digital dollar is not.

The e-CNY wallet — central-bank money in an app
A different asset class: Bitcoin and Ether are private tokens whose price floats. The e-CNY is the yuan itself — same unit, same value, just no paper.
A stablecoin borrows a currency's stability. A CBDC is that currency, in digital form. Neither of them is Bitcoin.
Unit 4 · Lesson 2 · Beyond the exam · Payment innovation

The form changes; the functions do not

Mobile wallets

Alipay and WeChat Pay move existing bank money instantly. In China they have largely replaced cash.

FedNow

The Fed's instant payment rail, live since 2023 — dollars settle in seconds, around the clock.

UPI

India's public instant-payment system — now the busiest retail payment network in the world.

Every one of these moves money faster. None of them invents a new unit of account: balances are still counted in dollars, yuan or rupees.

New plumbing, same three functions. When the exam asks what money does, the answer has not changed since the Yap stones.
Unit 4 · Lesson 2 · Before you move on

Three ways students lose points

1
Treating savings accounts as M1
Savings are M2: they store value well, but you cannot pay with them directly.
2
Putting bank reserves inside M1
Reserves belong to the monetary base only. They are not spendable by the public.
3
Mixing up commodity, commodity-backed and fiat money
Gold coins are commodity money; a gold-standard dollar is commodity-backed; today's dollar is backed by nothing but acceptance.
All three are vocabulary errors, not calculation errors. Precision with words is a graded skill on this exam.
