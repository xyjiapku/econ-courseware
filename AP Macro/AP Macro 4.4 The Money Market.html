Unit 4 · Lesson 6 · The big idea

Money is the one good whose price is an interest rate

A $20 note earns nothing

Hold it for a year and it is still $20. No dividend, no coupon, no rent — unlike every other asset you might own.

So holding it costs you

What it costs is the interest you gave up. That forgotten interest is the price of holding money.

And that price has a name

The nominal interest rate, i. Everything in this lesson follows from that single idea.

Money is not "free" to hold: its price is what you could have earned instead — the opportunity cost.

Unit 4 · Lesson 6 · Why MD slopes down

The trade-off, on a balance

Interest rate i = 2%
At low interest rates, keeping cash looks cheap — the money pan wins.
The higher i is, the heavier the interest pan — so you hold less money. That is the downward-sloping demand curve.
Unit 4 · Lesson 6 · Where money demand comes from

MV = PY — the reason money demand moves

MV = PY  →  M = PY / V

M

the money people hold

V

velocity — how many times a year a dollar is spent

P

the price level

Y

real output — so PY = nominal GDP

Read it as money demand

People hold money in proportion to the value of their transactions (PY) and inversely to how fast money moves (V).

So the shifters are the letters

Y up → hold more. P up → hold more. V up (faster payments) → hold less. The shifters are the letters themselves.

The same four letters are the four terms of the inflation identity — the next two pages put them to work.
Unit 4 · Lesson 6 · The same equation, read a second way

MV = PY, in growth rates

The last page read it as money demand. Read it in growth rates and it becomes a claim about inflation.
1
Start from the level version
M × V = P × Y
2
Take the percentage change of both sides
A product grows at the sum of its parts — so each side splits into two terms.
3
Four terms, one identity
%ΔM  +  %ΔV  =  %ΔP  +  %ΔY
4
Solve for the one we care about
%ΔP  =  %ΔM  +  %ΔV  −  %ΔY
Inflation is not the money growth rate — it is money growth, plus velocity change, minus real growth.
Unit 4 · Lesson 6 · Which of the four actually moves

Four terms, sixty-five years of data

All four terms, measured in the US over 65 years and drawn on one shared scale.

Y — real GDP: the calm one

It grows at its own pace, set by resources and technology — not by money. Average 3.1%, and it never ran outside −2.5% to +8.5% in 65 years.

V — velocity: not a behaviour

V is computed after the fact: V = PY / M. It is the reciprocal of money demand — how long the average dollar sits in a wallet. Its change averages −0.4%, but it is the wildest of the four.

M and P: the loud ones

Money growth averaged 6.9% and inflation 3.8% — and over long stretches the two lines travel together.

Long-run averages: 6.9 − 3.1 = 3.8 — money growth minus real growth is inflation. In 2020 money growth hit +24.5% while V fell −23.8% and inflation barely moved: the two cancelled. No single driver explains V (every correlation is under 0.5) — which is why the Fed abandoned M2 targeting in 1993.

Unit 4 · Lesson 6 · The supply side

Who supplies the money?

The Fed steers the quantity

It sets the monetary base. In this framework the quantity of money is what policy says it is — so MS is vertical.

Banks multiply it

Loans create deposits — last lesson's chain. The Fed sets the fuel, the banks do the multiplication.

Vertical means "whatever i is"

No matter the interest rate, the quantity supplied is fixed by policy. So the supply line does not slope — it shifts.

In the ample-reserves world this line behaves differently — that is 4.7's story.
Unit 4 · Lesson 6 · When the Fed did not set the supply

Under gold, nobody controlled the money supply

The quantity was mined, not decided

On the gold standard, money was claims on gold: the supply grew with gold output and flows, not with any committee's decision. The Fed's MS line could not be moved at will.

America's long deflation

From roughly 1873 to 1896 US prices fell about 1% a year for a generation. Debtors — above all farmers — were squeezed as the dollars they owed grew more valuable.

Free silver and 1896

The Free Silver movement demanded silver coinage to expand money. William Jennings Bryan's "Cross of Gold" speech (1896) became its anthem — and the election was fought over money.

The Wizard of Oz, read as allegory

Baum's 1900 tale is widely read as this debate in code: silver slippers (silver), the yellow brick road (gold), Oz (ounce), the Wizard as the politicians who promise but cannot deliver.

Two verdicts on the same institution: Hayek admired the discipline of commodity money and wanted money issued by competing private banks rather than a central bank; Keynes called gold "a barbarous relic" and blamed its deflationary grip for deepening the Depression.

The lesson: who controls the supply decides whether the price of money serves the economy or the metal.
Unit 4 · Lesson 6 · Target vs price

The Fed announces a target — the market does the pricing

What the FOMC announces

"We will keep the federal funds rate in a target range of 3.75–4.00%." That is a goal, published, not a price tag on the market.

What it cannot do

It cannot decree the rate at which two banks lend each other overnight — that price is made by the market, the same way a shop cannot decree what customers will pay.

How it gets there

In the scarce-reserves story it shifts MS (open market operations) until the market rate lands inside the range. In today's ample-reserves world it sets administered rates — IORB 3.90% as a floor (4.6–4.7).

So read every headline correctly: "the Fed raises rates" means it raises its target — implementation follows through the tools, and the market still has to trade there.

Announcement = target. Control = steering, with the tools of the day. The two are not the same thing.
Unit 4 · Lesson 6 · Real data (BLS, FOMC)

US inflation and the target rate, 2000–2026

The pattern: the Fed lags inflation — it tightened hard in 2022–23 as CPI hit 8.0%, eased in 2024–25 as inflation cooled, and in September 2026 hiked again (to 3.75–4.00%) with CPI back near 3.8%.
Unit 4 · Lesson 6 · Real data (PBoC, 2026)

China: the same levers, a different cycle

Quantity: RRR down for a decade

21.5% (2011) → 9.0% now; weighted average 6.2%.

Price: the 7-day reverse repo

China's policy rate: 1.40%, cut in May 2025.

LPR: the lending benchmark

3.0% (1-year) / 3.5% (5-year), flat 16 months — no cuts this year with CPI at +0.9% and the Fed hiking.

China: large-bank RRR (%), 1-year LPR (%) and the 7-day reverse repo policy rate (%). The RRR series starts earlier: the reserve system dates from 1984, while the 7-day repo became the policy rate only around 2015 and LPR became the loan benchmark after the 2019 reform.

Same toolkit — quantity plus an administered price. What differs is the cycle: China has eased in small steps for a decade, while the US ran from 0% to 5.5% and back.
Unit 4 · Lesson 6 · 中文讲清：谁把钱借给谁

隔夜拆借、7 天逆回购、给货币基金付息 — 到底在做什么交易？

① 隔夜拆借 — 银行之间"今天借、明天还"

营业结束，A 银行头寸不够、B 银行恰好闲着钱：B 借给 A 一晚，次日还本付息 — 这就是隔夜拆借利率。美国叫它联邦基金利率（两边都是银行，与政府无关）；中国的 DR001 要拿债券抵押，所以叫"质押式回购"。

② 7 天逆回购 — 央行向银行"放钱"的价格

"回购"＝先卖后买回，差价就是利息。逆回购是站在央行一侧：央行把钱借给银行 7 天，银行拿债券抵押，到期连本带息赎回。逆回购＝央行放钱，正回购＝央行收钱。央行天天用它放钱，于它成了中国的政策利率（现 1.40%）。

③ 给货币基金付息（ON RRP）— 美联储为什么"借"非银的钱

货币基金是普通人、企业放闲钱的"钱袋"，随时要能赎回。它们不是银行，不能在美联储开户，钱会乱找短期出路、把短期利率压到目标以下。于是美联储开个小窗口：让货币基金隔夜借钱给美联储并付息（现 3.75%）— 这就是 ON RRP，给非银也垫一层地板。

都是"借钱"，区别只在谁借给谁：银行 ↔ 银行 = 隔夜拆借；央行 → 银行 = 逆回购（放钱）；央行 ← 非银 = ON RRP；央行 ← 银行准备金 = IORB。

这些"利率"分别是什么？

名称是什么 · 谁决定
Federal funds rate　联邦基金利率银行之间隔夜拆借的价格 — 由市场交易形成，央行只能引导
Target range　目标区间 3.75–4.00%FOMC 宣布的政策目标，不是市场成交价
IORB / ON RRP　准备金利率 / 隔夜逆回购央行付息的两层地板：银行 3.90%，货币基金 3.75%
Discount rate　贴现率 4.00%银行直接向央行借钱的价，即"最后贷款人"利率
DR001　银行间隔夜质押回购利率中国货币市场的隔夜基准（2026 上半年均值 1.31%）
7-day reverse repo　7 天期逆回购 1.40%中国的政策利率，由中国人民银行宣布
LPR　贷款市场报价利率 3.0% / 3.5%银行贷款的定价基准，由报价行在政策利率上加/减点报出

Unit 4 · Lesson 6 · Before you move on

Three ways students lose points

1
Labelling the vertical axis wrong
The price on this graph is the nominal interest rate — not the price level, not the real rate.
2
Sloping the money supply
In this framework MS is vertical: policy fixes the quantity — and the line shifts, it never slopes.
3
Shifting MD when i changes
A change in i is a movement along MD. Only income, prices, technology or interest paid on money shift the curve.
All three are drawing errors — and drawing errors are where the marks are lost.
