One rate, three views: the Fed sets the policy rate, the money market draws the same rate, and Fisher — i = r + πe — hands it to loanable funds as the real rate. They cannot all pin it at once: when they disagree, πe or r gives.
In the ample regime, only IORB matters — shifting the supply of reserves changes nothing.

How they connect: the Fed creates reserves → banks lend and money is created → what is not spent becomes saving → saving plus foreign capital is the supply of loanable funds → which funds investment.
Money and reserves are stocks; loanable funds is a flow. Same rate runs through all three — different things being priced.

The policy rate is always trapped between two administered rates — then r = i − πe turns it into the rate that actually governs spending.
RelationWhich way it runsWhat it means

“Rate hikes fight inflation” is the short-run line; “high inflation goes with high nominal rates” is the long-run one. Same equation — never read the identity as a cause.
Unit 4 · Review · Integration 2b · Two ways to draw it

Two ways to draw the same policy

How the policy is drawnDoes it reach a new equilibrium?What you can then read off
Pin r away from r*, curves unmovedNo — it hangs there with a surplus or a shortageOnly how far from r*: the size of the push, not its consequences
Shift the supply of loanable fundsYes — at a lower r and a larger QThis one predicts: investment really does change
The gap is closed by banks creating money (reserves → deposits → loans) and by foreign capital — which is why the drawing is a supply shift, not a price held off its equilibrium.
Monetary easing → supply of loanable funds right → r down → investment up → AD up. Fiscal deficits move demand instead — different curve, different lesson.

Unit 4 · Extension · Closing the loop

From one open market purchase to real GDP

1
The Fed buys securities
Reserves rise and the price of bonds is bid up.
2
Bond prices up → yields down
By the inverse relation, the policy rate falls inside the target range.
3
Every other short rate follows
Banks will not lend below the Fed’s floor, so money-market rates move together.
4
Borrowing gets cheaper in real terms
Business investment and household housing and durables respond first.
5
Aggregate demand rises
More spending at every price level — the AD curve shifts right.
6
Real GDP, employment and the price level rise
And policy works with a lag — which is why timing is so hard.

The step textbooks skip: the Fed never sets a yield — it moves the price, and the rate follows.
Unit 4 · Extension · The inverse relation

Bond prices and interest rates move in opposite directions

You hold a bond paying a fixed $50 a year. New bonds now pay 10% — so nobody will pay you $1,000 for yours. Its price falls until its yield matches.
Market rate rPricevs face value
2%$1,058premium
5%$1,000at par
10%$913discount
Rate down → price up. Rate up → price down. Always. The coupon never moves — only the rate you discount it at.
The official wording: the price of previously issued bonds and interest rates on bonds are inversely related — and a new bond is simply issued at the new rate, so nothing has to adjust.
Unit 4 · Extension · Real data, 2000–2026

Twenty-six years of the same sentence

Yields down → prices up (2000–2020); yields up → prices down (2021–2023) — and the longer the bond, the harder it moves. In 2022 the 20+ year Treasury index lost 31.09% and the broad bond index 13.01% (Bloomberg indices). Source: FRED, quarterly averages.

Unit 4 · Review · Before you draw

The graph checklist that earns points

1
Label the axes first
i for the money and reserve markets; r for loanable funds. Quote the price on the vertical axis.
2
Draw the starting curves, then shift
Never draw only the new curve — the marker needs to see what moved.
3
Shift exactly what changed
One curve unless the question gives you two events. Arrow the direction.
4
Mark the new equilibrium
Label it (i₂, r₂, Q₂) and show the move with a dotted line to each axis.
5
Say the answer in words
“The rate rises because demand shifted right while supply did not.” A correct graph with no sentence loses points.
