Recap & Introduction

Past three lessons:

• Scarcity → limited resources, unlimited wants

• Every choice → opportunity cost (the next best alternative foregone)

• Economics = study of choice under scarcity

Today:

How does society organize scarce resources?

Every society → three basic questions

Different answers → different economic systems

THREE BASIC QUESTIONS

What to produce?

Scarce resources → cannot produce everything

Which goods & services? In what quantities?

Examples:

• Food vs weapons (steel)

• Consumer goods vs capital goods

• Healthcare vs national defense

Fun story: Jin Yong vs Chen Yi — "trousers or atomic bombs?"

THREE BASIC QUESTIONS

How to produce?

After what → how: which combination of resources?

More labor or more capital (machinery)?

→ labor-intensive vs capital-intensive

Examples:

• Cheap coal vs clean natural gas for electricity

• Local production vs outsourcing abroad

THREE BASIC QUESTIONS

For whom to produce?

Who gets the output? → problem of distribution

Who receives and consumes what we produce?

Examples:

• Free healthcare: who pays, who receives?

• Tax the rich to help the poor?

• Wages for workers vs profits for capital owners

ECONOMIC SYSTEMS

What is an economic system?

An economic system = the set of:

• Institutions (rules & organizations)

• Mechanisms (processes)

that decide resource allocation & distribution

Two key differences between systems:

1. Who owns the resources?

2. Who decides what/how/for whom?

Four main types:

Traditional → Market → Command → Mixed

ECONOMIC SYSTEMS

Type 1: Traditional economy

Based on customs, habits, traditions

Examples: remote villages in less developed regions

Key features:

• Occupations passed down parent → child

• Very slow change, little innovation

• Agriculture, hunting, fishing, gathering

• Barter, little trade

ECONOMIC SYSTEMS

Type 2: Market economy / Capitalism

Also called capitalist or free-market economy

• Private ownership of resources

• Decentralized decisions by consumers & producers

• Price mechanism (supply & demand) coordinates

Key features:

• Consumer sovereignty: "voting with dollars"

• Profit motive → competition → efficiency

• Limited government: "laissez-faire"

"The invisible hand" — Adam Smith

ECONOMIC SYSTEMS

Type 3: Command / Planned economy

Also called planned, socialist, or communist economy

• Public (state) ownership of resources

• Centralized decisions by the government

• Central planning authority sets targets, allocates resources

Key features:

• Planners, not consumers, answer the three questions

• Little consumer choice

Examples: former USSR, North Korea

ECONOMIC SYSTEMS

Type 4: Mixed economy

= a mix of market + command

• Private sector: private ownership, free markets

• Public sector: government provides public goods, regulates

Why mixed?

• Markets sometimes fail (pollution, public goods)

• Government can improve: defence, roads, schools, welfare

Almost all real-world economies are mixed.

e.g. USA, UK, China — different mixes
