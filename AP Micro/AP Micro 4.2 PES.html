THE FAMINE · 饥荒

Bad harvest. Empty shelves. Raise the price?

One region, one failed harvest — two voices arguing.

The situation

The harvest fails. Grain is short: queues, empty shelves, anger. The government must decide — let the price rise, or cap it?

"Let it rise"

Higher price pulls in more supply → more people fed.

"Cap the price"

Cap it — or the poorest cannot eat.

Your call? Hold it — the famine comes back on the second-to-last case page, with today's tool in hand.

Voice A assumes supply can respond to price. Steep or flat — nobody has checked yet.

Voice B: the problem is money, not quantity.

Both argue about the same thing: how much supply can answer a price.

SAME PRICE RISE · 直觉

Same price rise — two very different answers

Price rises $30 → $36 (a fixed +20%). Watch how differently quantity supplied answers.

The experiment

Hold the price rise fixed (+20%) — the only thing that differs is the steepness of supply.

① Steep supply

Quantity 5 → 5.3: the same +20% price rise brings hardly any extra supply.

② Flat supply

Quantity 5 → 11: the same +20% price rise brings a lot of extra supply.

Same price rise, very different quantity answer. Everything else equal — the number that measures this gap is on the next page.

DEFINITION · 定义

Meet PES

We need a number that says how strongly sellers respond to a price change. Here it is.

1 · The idea — PES is measured by the percentage change in quantity supplied divided by the percentage change in price — the responsiveness of Qs to a change in price.

2 · The formula

PES= %ΔQs%ΔP = ΔQ ÷ QΔP ÷ P

3 · Read the number. PES is positive — price up, quantity supplied up (the law of supply). A PES of 2.6 means a 1% price rise extends supply by 2.6%.

4 · Why bother? Famine, pig cycle, tax incidence — all decided by this number.

CALCULATION · 计算

Work out a PES — step by step

Read the question. Work out your answer. Then reveal the four steps.

The question

Price rises from $10 to $12. Quantity supplied rises from 100 to 130.
Find the PES — and say whether supply is elastic.

1 · %ΔQs — change ÷ original × 100: (130 − 100) ÷ 100 × 100 = +30%

2 · %ΔP — same method: (12 − 10) ÷ 10 × 100 = +20%

3 · Divide — PES = +30% ÷ +20% = 1.5

4 · Read it. PES > 1 → elastic: a 1% price rise pulls out 1.5% more supply. Price falls? Both changes go negative — the ratio stays positive.

5 · Keep going — two more, exam style: ① Q 10 000 → 4 000 when price $5 → $4. ② Q 200 → 210 when price $10 → $14. Work both, then reveal.

✓ ① 3 · elastic — −60% ÷ −20%; both fall, the ratio stays positive. ✓ ② 0.125 · inelastic — +5% ÷ +40%: price quadruples, supply barely moves.

PUZZLE · 猜想

Two lines through the origin — guess both PES values

Both supply curves start at the origin. Price rises from $1 to $5. What is the PES on each?

The set-up

S₁: P = Q (shallow). S₂: P = 2Q (twice as steep). Price $1 → $5 = +400%. Guess each PES first.

① On S₁ (P = Q)

Q goes 1 → 5, so %ΔQ = +400% → PES = 400% ÷ 400% = 1.

② On S₂ (P = 2Q)

Q goes 0.5 → 2.5 — also +400% → PES = 1 again, though the line is twice as steep.

The rule: any straight line through the origin has PES = 1 at every point. Steepness does not matter — only proportionality does. Proof below.

Why (four lines): P = k×Q → Q = P ÷ k → Q′ ÷ Q = P′ ÷ P (the k cancels) → PES = %ΔQ ÷ %ΔP = 1. A line not through the origin has a different PES at each point.

FIVE DEGREES · 五种

Five degrees — from 0 to ∞

Click a card to see its curve. Read the slope first, then the number — they run from the least responsive to the most.

PES = 0 · perfectly inelastic

Vertical — quantity supplied cannot change at all.

Examples: seats for tonight's film; land in the centre of a city.

The rule: the flatter the curve, the bigger the PES — the same price rise moves quantity further.

DETERMINANT 2 · 可储存

Can it be kept until the price moves?

Stock is supply that has already been produced — it can be released the moment price rises.
Price rises. Can the seller answer today?
YES — it can be stored
▼

Sell from stock → ELASTIC

Price up → release stock; price down → hold it back. Oil in tanks, grain in silos, tinned food.

NO — it perishes
▼

Today's offer is all there is → INELASTIC

Fresh milk, strawberries, a haircut, tonight's seat — whatever is offered today is all there is.

Rice, 2008: output was a record high, yet prices tripled — hoarding and export bans. When Japan released stock, prices fell at once. China's frozen-pork reserve is the same idea: buy low, release high.

OTHER FACTORS · 其他因素

Other factors

Every one of them asks the same question: how easily can more be supplied?

Spare capacity

Machines used a few hours a day → switch them on. All busy → new plant and new land.

Cost of altering supply

Cheap to change output → easy to expand. New plant or scarce specialists → hard.

Mobility of factors

Land, labour and capital that can switch to this good quickly — inputs other goods also want.

Natural resources

A depleting resource is harder to expand — oil wells, rare earths → inelastic.

Time is the master switch: momentary < short run < long run — every factor above loosens with time.

WHY IT MATTERS · 意义

Two people who really need PES

One is deciding whether to expand. One is deciding who pays.

The firm 💼

Demand jumps — can you actually supply it? Elastic → take the sales. Inelastic → customers go elsewhere.

The government 🏛️

Incidence needs both elasticities. Elastic supply → firms escape a tax. Inelastic supply → firms cannot escape it.

And for a famine? If supply cannot respond in time, a higher price only moves money.

REAL CASE · 猪周期

The pig cycle — a lag you can see

Real live-hog prices, 2019–2025. Demand barely moves — the supply response takes about ten months.

Ten months from decision to meat. Price ↑ → more sows → pigs flood the market → price ↓ → sows cut → shortage again. 2019–2025: $38.71 → $12.30 (−70%).

POLICY · 平滑周期

Fighting a lag you cannot remove

Nobody can make a pig grow faster. So policy works on the information and the stock.

A government reserve-pork counter: the state sells from stock when the price runs high.

Buy low, release high

The state buys when the price is low and sells when it is high — the stock farms cannot hold.

Target the herd, not the price

A sow-inventory band (normal ≈ 43 m sows, floor 40 m) warns farmers before they all expand together.

Lock a price · share the numbers

Hog futures fix a price today; publishing the sow count means fewer farmers join the flood.

The lag (≈10 months) cannot be removed — the aim is to stop everyone reacting at once.

BACK TO THE FAMINE · 回答

So — should the price have been capped?

Build it: the market, then the shortage, then the grain that arrives from elsewhere.

⓪ Before the famine

Grain: D and S cross at $30 / 5 units; supply is steep.

① The harvest fails → D jumps

Price $30 → $34.8 (+16%) but food supplied only 5 → 5.2 (+5%): PES ≈ 0.3.

The rise moves money, it does not grow grain — and invites hoarding.

② Grain arrives from elsewhere

Imports at $33: food 5.2 → 5.6, price falls back — from moving grain, not growing it.

The answer: let the price rise some — but cap the extremes, stop hoarding, and bring grain in fast.

A WAY OF THINKING

Three ways to see the same famine

See the mountain → see it as not a mountain → see it as a mountain again.
1

No economics — the moral answer

"Prices must not rise — the poor would starve." Intuition and fairness. It protects people, but it cannot say what the price rise would actually do.

2

A little economics — the clever answer

"Let prices rise! A higher price calls out more supply." Simple, confident — and it sounds anti-moral. It treats supply as if it always answers.

3

Deeper economics — the full answer

Short-run PES ≈ 0 and hoarding: allow some rise, cap the extremes, import fast. The moral goal survives — with science under it.

A caution. Some popular writers — Xue Zhaofeng (薛兆丰) is the classic case — like using first-pass economics to dress up anti-common-sense, anti-moral conclusions.

Economics is a tool for understanding reality. Counter-intuitive ≠ counter-moral.
