DEFINITION · 定义

Meet PED

A number for how strongly buyers react to price. Here it is.

1 · The idea — price elasticity of demand (PED) measures how responsive the quantity demanded is to a change in price. CED: "%Δ quantity demanded ÷ %Δ price".

2 · The formula

PED= %ΔQd%ΔP = ΔQ ÷ QΔP ÷ P = ΔQΔP × PQ

3 · Read the number. PED is negative (price up → Qd down), so economists compare the size with 1. Elastic above 1, inelastic below 1, unit elastic at 1 — always for the price range of the given change. A PED of −2 means a 1% price rise cuts Qd by 2%.

4 · Why bother? Because the same price cut can raise or sink a firm's revenue — you saw it in the price war. PED is the number that predicts which one happens, and it guides firms' pricing and government taxes.

CALCULATION · 计算

Work out a PED — step by step

Price falls from $10 to $9, and Qd rises from 200 to 240. Four small steps.

1 · %ΔQd — change ÷ original × 100: (240 − 200) ÷ 200 × 100 = +20%

2 · %ΔP — same method: (9 − 10) ÷ 10 × 100 = −10%

3 · Divide — PED = +20% ÷ (−10%) = −2

4 · Read it. The minus sign is just the law of demand. The size 2: a 1% price change moves Qd by 2% — buyers react strongly.

FIVE DEGREES · 谱系

Five degrees of elasticity

Inelastic (< 1): Qd changes by a smaller percentage than price. Typical of necessities — salt, petrol, medicine. Price and revenue move in the same direction.

CONTRAST · 对比

Same price rise, two very different curves

Both demand curves start at $30 / 5 cups. Raise the price and watch how differently Qd responds.

Steep curve → inelastic

Buyers barely move: Qd falls by a smaller % than the price rose. |PED| < 1.

Shallow curve → elastic

Buyers flee: Qd falls by a bigger % than the price rose. |PED| > 1.

Careful: "steep/shallow" only compares curves on the same scale. Always check the axes before judging.

DETERMINANT 1 · 因素

The main factor: availability of substitutes

CED 2.3 (MKT-3.E.4): PED depends on factors such as the availability of substitutes — the closer the substitutes, the more elastic.

A close substitute exists → elastic

A close substitute of similar quality and price → demand is elastic: a rise makes consumers switch.

No close substitute → inelastic

No close substitute → inelastic: quantity will not fall much — no suitable alternative.

DETERMINANT 2 · 因素

Second: the proportion of income spent on it

A small share of the buyer’s budget → inelastic; a large share → elastic.

Salt — a small share of income

$1 → $1.5 is a rise of 50% — but only $0.5 more. Barely noticed → demand is inelastic.

A new car — a large share of income

$1,000,000 → $1,500,000 is the same 50% — but $500,000 more. Buyers postpone → demand is elastic.

Same 50%, different money — $0.5 vs $500,000. What matters is the share of income the rise takes.

OTHER INFLUENCES · 因素

Other influences

Five more — all working through substitutes.
  • Necessity or luxury? — Luxuries (gold) can be left → elastic; necessities (soap) → inelastic.
  • Addictive or delayable? — Addictive (cigarettes) → inelastic; delayable → elastic.
  • Complements — a cheap add-on to an expensive good (charger + phone) → inelastic.
  • The time period — more time to find alternatives → more elastic in the long run.
  • How narrowly the market is defined — the narrower the market, the more substitutes → the more elastic (one brand of tea vs tea in general).

How TR moves as the price falls — the total revenue test

1 · Watch the rectangle

Press Play ▶ — the price slides from top to bottom. Watch only the area.

2 · What did the area do?

It grew, peaked in the middle, then shrank.

3 · Turn it into PED (the test)

TR rising → ELASTIC · TR falling → INELASTIC · peak → PED = 1.

4 · Conclusion

Straight line: PED falls as Q rises — elastic, PED = 1 in the middle (Q = 5.5), then inelastic. Not the slope.

P × Q = the rectangle
Q × TR = the area, as a curve
WHY PED = 1 · 推导

What shape has PED = 1 everywhere?

Start from the test you just used: TR = P × Q.

1 · The condition

PED = 1 → %ΔQd = %ΔP: a price rise cuts quantity by the same percentage.

2 · So revenue cannot move

TR = P × Q: if P rises 10% and Q falls 10%, the changes cancel → TR constant.

3 · Solve for Q

TR = $600 → Q = 600 ÷ P: $4 → 150, $3 → 200, $2 → 300. Halve the price, double the quantity.

4 · The shape

A rectangular hyperbola: every P × Q rectangle has the same area; PED = 1 at every point → its TR curve is horizontal.

Straight line vs hyperbola: on a straight demand curve PED = 1 at one point only (the middle); the hyperbola is the curve where it holds everywhere.
