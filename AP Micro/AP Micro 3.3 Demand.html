From a physics law to the law of demand · 需求定律
DR. JIA
DEFINITION · 需求是什么

What is demand?

A need is not demand. A want is not demand. Only a want you can and will pay for is demand.
DEMAND = willingness + ability to pay, at a given price, over a period of time.

A desire → ability to pay (money in hand) → willingness to pay (you choose to spend it) → demand.

Need
must-have to survive — demand only if you can and will pay
Want
anything you desire — unlimited, and it ignores the price
Demand
a want with money behind it — effective demand

Common mistake

Demand is not unlimited, while wants are: demand is limited by income and by the price.

HOW A LAW IS BORN · TWO LAWS, ONE LOGIC

Side by side — physics and economics

Two different sciences. One identical recipe. That's why the lines and the predictions look the same.

🔬 Hooke's Law

① Control: same spring

② Change: force F

③ Record: stretch x

④ Fit: F = kx · slope = k (stiffness)

Same shape, every time you stretch a spring.

📈 Law of Demand

① Control: ceteris paribus — other things equal

② Change: price P

③ Record: quantity demanded Qd

④ Fit: Qd falls as P rises · negative slope

Same shape, every time the canteen raises a price.

Notice ① — economists even borrow the word control from physics: ceteris paribus. Remember it — it decides whether the curve moves along or shifts.

AP SKILL ⓪ · 平移的由来

Same price — more bought

Some other factor changes (income, tastes, the price of a related good). The price stays at ¥20, yet buyers now want more cups. Watch what that does to the curve.

Step by step

① Start from D: at ¥20 buyers want 7 cups.

② Something else changes — not the price. At the same ¥20 they now want 10 cups.

③ Every price gives a bigger quantity, so the whole curve has to move right to D′.

The test: did the good's own price change? If yes → move along the curve. If no, and quantity still changed → the curve shifted.

FACTORS ① INCOME · 收入变化

Income changes — normal vs inferior goods

The most important determinant. But the direction depends on the type of good.

Normal goods

Income ↑ → demand rises (shift RIGHT)

e.g. milk tea, new phones, eating out

Inferior goods

Income ↑ → demand falls (shift LEFT)

e.g. instant noodles, bus rides, second-hand clothes

“Inferior” is not an insult — it just means buyers switch away from it when they get richer.

Memorise the pair: normal good → income ↑ = shift right; inferior good → income ↑ = shift left.

FACTORS ② RELATED GOODS · 相关品价格

Substitutes & complements

The price of a related good shifts your curve too — in opposite directions.

Substitutes

Goods that replace each other: Coke & Pepsi, tea & coffee, bus & metro.

Substitute's price ↑ → our demand ↑ (shift RIGHT)

Pepsi gets dearer → buyers switch to Coke

Complements

Goods used together: game console & games, printer & ink, phones & apps.

Complement's price ↑ → our demand ↓ (shift LEFT)

Consoles get dearer → fewer games are wanted too

Memorise the pair: substitute's price ↑ = shift right; complement's price ↑ = shift left. The two directions are opposites — AP loves this.

FACTORS ③ THE REST · 其余因素

Advertising, population, tastes — and more

Small but heavily tested — each one shifts the whole curve.

📢 Advertising

A successful campaign makes the brand popular → demand shifts right.

👥 Number of buyers

More people (or more students in school) → demand shifts right.

👗 Tastes & fashion

Trendy → right; out of fashion → left.

…and more

Weather (a hot summer → ice cream) · Expectations of future prices (expect a price rise → buy today) · Special days (Valentine's → roses) — each one shifts the curve.

All of these move the whole curve — never a movement along it.

AP SKILL ① · 沿线

Change in quantity demanded — move along the curve

The canteen puts the price of milk tea up by ¥5. Nothing else has changed — not your pocket money, not your taste. You simply buy fewer cups.

Extension / contraction

Price ↓ → slide down D: an extension of demand.
Price ↑ → slide up D: a contraction of demand.

You never leave the curve — the same D, another point.

Common mistake

If the price changed, say quantity demanded fell — never demand fell.

AP SKILL ② · 平移

Change in demand — the whole curve shifts

The milk tea price stays at ¥20, but something else changes — your pocket money rises. At every price you now want more, so the whole curve moves right.

Increase / decrease in demand

D shifts right → an increase in demand. D shifts left → a decrease in demand. Only the good's own price moves you along D; every other cause shifts the whole curve.

At every single price the quantity bought is now different — that is what a shift means.

Same price, different quantity. Nothing on the price axis moved — the curve did.

DETERMINANTS · 需求的决定因素

What shifts the curve?

Seven things move the whole demand curve — and the good's own price is not one of them.

Common mistake

A shift is caused by anything except the price of the good itself. If the sentence mentions the good's own price → it is a movement, never a shift.

WORDING · 术语对照

Extension / contraction — or increase / decrease?

Two pairs of words look alike. One moves you along the curve — the other moves the whole curve.
WordingCauseOn the graph
Extension of demand — also called an increase in quantity demandedprice ↓slide down along the same D
Contraction of demand — also called a decrease in quantity demandedprice ↑slide up along the same D
Increase / decrease in demanda determinant changesthe whole curve shifts D → D′

Say it the right way

Price changed → extension / contraction — also called an increase / decrease in quantity demanded.
Determinant changed → increase / decrease in demand — the curve shifts.

Common mistake

Income rises and people buy more at every price → that is an increase in demand (shift), not an “extension”. Extension only ever means a price change.

WHY ↓ REASON ① · 替代效应

Reason ① The substitution effect

Coffee goes from ¥20 to ¥30; tea is still ¥20. Nothing else changed — coffee just became dear compared with tea, so buyers switch.

Coffee

its own price rose → buyers move up its own D:
a contraction of quantity demanded

Tea

its substitute got dearer → the whole curve moves right:
an increase in demand

Two things at once. Coffee's own price moved → contraction along its own D. Tea's own price did not move, but its substitute got dearer → its curve shifts: an increase in demand.

WHY ↓ REASON ② · 收入效应

Reason ② The income effect

Pocket money is still ¥100 a week. Milk tea goes from ¥20 to ¥25. Nothing about your income changed — but your money now buys less.

Key idea

A price rise makes your real income (purchasing power) fall — the same money buys fewer units, so quantity demanded falls.

Both effects push the same way when the price rises.

THE CLASSIC TRAP · 经典陷阱

Income effect ≠ change in income

Both sentences mention money. Only one of them moves the curve.
The income effectA change in income
caused by a change in the good's pricea determinant changing (pocket money really changes)
you move along the same curve Dthe whole curve shifts (D → D′)
say: “quantity demanded falls”say: “demand falls”

“Income effect” is not the same thing as “income changed”: one is a movement, the other is a shift.

WHY ↓ REASON ③ · 边际效用递减

Reason ③ Diminishing marginal utility

Each extra unit is worth less — and that is why the curve slopes down.

① Each unit worth less

The first cup on a hot day is worth a lot; the fourth far less. So buyers only take extra units at a lower price.

② The curve is marginal benefit

The height of D at any quantity = what the last unit is worth. Falling MB → falling willingness to pay → downward slope.

③ Three reasons, one direction

Substitution · income · diminishing MU — all three say: price ↑ → Qd ↓.

That is the law of demand, and now you can explain it three ways — not just state it.

CETERIS PARIBUS · 控制变量

Four sliders — which one moves the curve?

Four sliders. Only price slides you along the curve; the other three move the whole curve. Ceteris paribus is the key.

① Price (along the curve)

② Income (shifts the curve)

③ Complement price

④ Substitute price

Slide each one — exactly one of them does NOT move the curve.
