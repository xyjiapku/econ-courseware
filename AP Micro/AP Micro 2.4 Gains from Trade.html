Step 5 · Where do they agree?

Find the deal zone

Put the two questions together. By comparative advantage, A hunts, B picks — now A has rabbits to sell, and B wants to buy. At what price does the deal happen?

Perfect match at 3, 4, 5: A sells (price ≥ his OC 2) and B buys (price ≤ his OC 6).
The terms of trade must lie between the two opportunity costs.

Special case

What if their OCs are identical?

Two producers, but one is just a scaled copy of the other:

PersonRabbits per dayFruits per day
X6020
Y3010

Q · Compute the OCs. Does anyone have a comparative advantage?

PersonOC of 1 rabbitOC of 1 fruit
X1/3 fruit3 rabbits
Y1/3 fruit3 rabbits
Identical OCs → nobody has a comparative advantage → no gains from trade.
Trading with an identical partner is like trading with yourself — swapping 1 rabbit for 1/3 fruit changes nothing.
Key idea: gains from trade exist only when the opportunity costs differ.

Summary

Today in six blanks

① Trade makes both parties better off — this is mutual benefit.

② Specialise where your opportunity cost is lowest — that is comparative advantage.

③ A deal price must lie between the two opportunity costs.

④ Absolute advantage = more output; comparative advantage = lower OC.

⑤ Identical OCs → no gains from trade — don't trade with your clone.

⑥ England & Portugal: specialise by comparative advantage, trade at a price between the two OCs — both consume beyond their PPC.
