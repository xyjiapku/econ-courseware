TWO SURPLUSES · 一图

One market, two triangles

A normal market: demand and supply cross at P = $30, Q = 5. Build the picture in three steps.

1 · The market — D and S cross at P = $30, Q = 5. Only 5 units get made and sold.

2 · Consumer surplus — above $30, below D: ½ × 5 × 25 = $62.5.

3 · Producer surplus — below $30, above S: ½ × 5 × 25 = $62.5.

Total economic surplus = CS + PS = $125. This is what the market hands to buyers and sellers together.

UNDER-PRODUCTION · 不足

Too little output — the wedge nobody gets

Output stuck at Q = 3 instead of 5: the units between 3 and 5 are worth more than they cost — and they are never made.

① Only 3 units are made. Buyers pay $40 for the third unit — it costs just $20 to make.

② Both surpluses shrink: CS = $22.5, PS = $82.5 → total $105, not $125.

③ The gap: the wedge between D and S from 3 to 5 = ½ × 2 × 20 = $20 — value nobody receives.

OVER-PRODUCTION · 过量

Too much output — where does the loss hide?

Force output up to Q = 8 (best is Q = 5): consumer surplus grows, producer surplus turns negative, and the two cancel down to one triangle.

① Output forced to 8. Buyers will only pay D(8) = $15, while the last unit costs S(8) = $45.

② Consumer surplus grows: the area between D and the price line = $160 — far bigger than the efficient $62.5.

③ Producer surplus: squeezed to +$10 on the left, and every unit beyond q = 2 loses (MC > price): −$90.

④ Net: 160 + 10 − 90 = $80 against the efficient $125 → the shortfall $45 is exactly the triangle between D and S from 5 to 8.

Price only moves surplus around

Hold Q at the efficient 5 and drag the price: buyers hand sellers ΔP × Q, and the parts that spill outside the triangles cancel.

Allocative efficiency: the quantity where the marginal benefit of the last unit = its marginal cost (MB = MC). At Q = 5 that holds, so total surplus is the largest it can be — no price alone can improve on it.

TWO EFFICIENCIES · 两种效率

Allocative vs productive efficiency

Two different questions: what to make is answered by the market; how to make is answered by the PPC.

Allocative · WHAT to make — decided by the market equilibrium (D ∩ S): the last unit's MB = MC, total surplus is max.

Productive · HOW to make — a point on the PPC: no wasted resources; more of one good now costs some of the other.

Different jobs: the market picks the point (allocative); the PPC shows it wastes nothing (productive).
