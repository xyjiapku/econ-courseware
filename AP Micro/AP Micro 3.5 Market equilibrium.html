DISEQUILIBRIUM · 失衡

When the price is wrong: shortage & surplus

A market is in disequilibrium when Qd and Qs do not match — the gap does the pushing.

Below ¥30 → a shortage

Qd is bigger than Qs: excess demand. Some buyers miss out, so the price is pushed UP.

Above ¥30 → a surplus

Qs is bigger than Qd: excess supply. Cups go unsold, so sellers cut the price DOWN.

The size of the gap is the size of the push. Only at ¥30 is the gap zero — nothing pushes, and the price stays put.

4 · At ¥30 the gap closes — market clears.

Below ¥30 — shortage

1 · At ¥20: Qd (7) > Qs (3) → 4 miss out.

2 · Buyers bid up → sellers raise the price.

3 · Qd contracts, Qs extends.

4 · At ¥30 the gap closes — market clears.

THE PRICE MECHANISM · 机制

Price does the organising

Nobody plans it. The price carries the message, and resources follow.

It is a signal — and an incentive

If buyers want more, the price rises. A higher price means more profit, so firms produce more — and they hire more labour, capital and land to do it.

It also rations

When supply falls short, the price is pushed up — and the people who end up with the product are those able to pay the higher price.

That is how a market answers the three big questions: what to produce, how to produce it, and for whom.

REVISION ① · 回顾

What shifts demand?

Every condition, with the direction it pushes the curve — green = increase (→), red = decrease (←).

Conditions of DEMAND

① Income — normal or inferior decides the direction

  • Normal good: income ↑ → D increase · income ↓ → D decrease
  • Inferior good: the opposite — income ↑ → D decrease

② Price of related goods — substitutes & complements

  • Substitute dearer ↑ → D increase (buyers switch in)
  • Complement dearer ↑ → D decrease (the pair costs more)

③ Other factors — especially expectations

  • Expect the price to rise → buy now → D increase
  • Also: tastes · advertising · population · seasons
REVISION ② · 回顾

What shifts supply?

Every condition, with the direction it pushes the curve — green = increase (→), red = decrease (←).

Conditions of SUPPLY

① Costs — wages, materials, energy, rent

  • Costs ↑ → S decrease · costs ↓ → S increase

② Taxes and subsidies

  • A tax makes production dearer → S decrease
  • A subsidy makes it cheaper → S increase

③ Expectations and other factors

  • Expect the price to rise → hold stock back → S decrease
  • Also: technology · weather · number of sellers

WORDS THAT EARN MARKS · 词汇

Along the curve, or the whole curve?

Two pairs of words look alike. One moves you along the curve — the other moves the whole curve.

Increase / decrease in demand — the curve moves

An increase in demand = the whole demand curve shifts right. A decrease in demand = it shifts left. Same wording for supply.

Trigger: a determinant changed — income, tastes, costs, tax…

Extension / contraction — you move along it

An extension of demand — also called an increase in quantity demanded. A contraction — a decrease in quantity demanded.

Trigger: the product's own price changed — nothing else.

Shift D right → the price rises → this causes an extension of supply.
Shift S right → the price falls → this causes an extension of demand.

One line to remember: curves shift, quantities move along.

Blue arrow = the curve shifts (an increase in demand) · green arrow = movement along the curve (an extension of supply)

WHY THIS TOOL · 为什么用模型

Two ways to answer the same question

The council raises the property tax on rented flats. What happens to the rent?

What most people would say

The tax goes up, so landlords will put the rent up to cover it — and tenants pay.

It sounds fair, and it sounds obvious. But ask two questions:

· Why aren't rents already as high as the market will bear?
· Is your landlord being kind to you?

What the model says

① The tax is on owning property — not on letting it.

② An empty flat now costs the owner money → some vacant flats come onto the market → supply increases →

③ Demand is unchanged → rent falls ↓, quantity rented rises ↑.

⇒ A property tax does not raise rents — it can lower them.

○ before  →  ● after

WHY THIS TOOL · 反直觉

When the model disagrees with your guess

Two more cases where the obvious answer is wrong — turn each card over to see why.

The case for protecting tenants

The law says a tenancy renews automatically and a tenant cannot be evicted.

Tenants are safer — so renting out must be more attractive now, surely?

The model  ○ before → ● after

① Landlords see more hassle and risk → some keep flats empty → supply decreases ←

② Demand unchanged → rent rises ↑, fewer flats let.

⇒ tenants end up worse off. The lasting fix: build more.

A bumper harvest

The harvest is huge — far more grain than last year.

Farmers must be delighted: more to sell means more money, doesn't it?

The model  ○ before → ● after

① More is offered at every price → supply increases →

② Demand unchanged → price falls ↓, quantity sold rises ↑.

⇒ each bag sells for less.

Science usually starts by contradicting intuition. Heavy objects do not fall faster; the sun does not go round the earth. A model earns its keep when it tells you something your instinct would miss.
