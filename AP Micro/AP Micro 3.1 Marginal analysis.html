Step 2

Utility and Marginal Utility

效用与边际效用

Total utility (TU)

Utility = the satisfaction a person gets from a good.
Total utility = the satisfaction from everything consumed so far.

Measured in utils. From the Coke game: the early cans add a lot, and TU climbs all the way to 60 utils.

Marginal utility (MU)

Marginal utility = the extra satisfaction from consuming one more unit.
MU = change in TU ÷ change in quantity

Marginal always asks: what does one more unit change? Can 5 → 4 utils · can 6 → 0 · can 7 → −4.

TU is the total; MU is the step. The blue curve is the running total, the orange bars are the steps that build it.
Step ① — the total: how happy is he altogether?
Step 3

The Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility

边际效用递减
As a person consumes more units of a good, the marginal utility of each additional unit falls — while total utility keeps rising until MU turns negative. Both are measured in utils, so they share one axis and are directly comparable.

Blue = total utility

Rises to 60 utils at can 6, then falls. It starts at the origin, because 0 cans = 0 utils.

Orange = marginal utility

A falling line: 20, 16, 12, 8, 4, 0, −4, −8. Same axis, so you can compare its height with TU directly.

Careful: "MU is falling" does not mean "TU is falling" — TU falls only after MU goes below zero. The two green dots mark the turning point: MU = 0 and TU at its maximum.

Extension

From Labour to Marginal Utility

劳动价值论 → 边际效用价值论
For a century economists asked: where does value come from? Two answers — and the second one is the answer AP expects.

1 · Labour theory of value

  • Assumes value is objective and fixed — inside the good, same for everyone.
  • If it is fixed, something must measure it.
  • The measure ≈ the good's labour cost (Smith, Ricardo, Marx).
  • Equal labour → equal value.
  • ❌ Fails: the paradox of value — water essential & cheap, diamonds useless & dear.

2 · Marginal utility theory of value

  • Rejects it: value is subjective — in the buyer's mind, not in the good.
  • So it is not fixed: it differs by person, and falls as you consume more.
  • ✅ Labour alone proves nothing — heavy labour can still be worth nothing.
  • Water abundant → low MU → cheap. Diamonds scarce → high MU → dear.
Objective & fixed ↔ subjective & changeable — only the second solves the water–diamond puzzle.
AP link: willingness to pay = marginal utility → demand slopes down.
Step ① — the old answer: value = labour.
Summary

Key Ideas

总结 · 分步填空

1 · What "marginal" means

Marginal = the unit — "one more or one less", never all-or-nothing.

2 · Diminishing marginal utility

More units → smaller MU. TU rises while MU > 0, peaks where MU = , and falls once MU < .

3 · The principle behind everything

Rational people think at the — they weigh the extra benefit against the extra cost of one more unit.

4 · What AP tests here

A consumer's willingness to pay depends on the the good gives — not on its total usefulness. (Why water is cheap and diamonds are dear.)

5 · The decision rule

MB usually , MC usually . Keep going while MB MC, and stop where MB = MC — there total net benefit is at its maximum (in the Coke story: can ).

6 · Why it is useful in real life

The same rule answers: how many hours to study, whether to take one more hour of overtime, how many units a firm should produce, whether one more kilometre of road is worth building. In every case ask: is the extra benefit than the extra cost?
